= Module 1: Discover and Benchmark a Validated Model
:navtitle: Module 1: Discover & Benchmark
:toc: macro
:toclevels: 1
:icons: font

Before you serve a large language model, you have to choose the _right_ one for your workload and your hardware budget. In this module you'll use the RHOAI **AI hub** catalog and its **model performance** view to compare Red Hat AI validated models by latency under a chatbot workload -- and use that comparison to select `{model_name}`.

== Learning objectives

By the end of this module, you'll be able to:

* Browse the AI hub catalog and filter to Red Hat AI validated models
* Configure the model performance view with a workload scenario and hardware constraint
* Compare candidate models by time-to-first-token (TTFT) latency
* Read a model card and explain why benchmark latency and hardware footprint together drive model selection

== Exercise 1: Browse the validated model catalog

. In the RHOAI dashboard left sidebar, expand **AI hub** and click **Catalog**.
+
image::04-ai-hub-catalog.png[AI hub catalog,link=self,window=blank,width=700]

. Filter the catalog to **Red Hat AI validated models**. These are models Red Hat has tested and packaged for serving on the platform.
+
NOTE: The exact set of catalog models depends on your RHOAI release, so the list you see may differ from the screenshots.

=== Verify

* The catalog shows a set of Red Hat AI validated models
* Each model tile shows its provider and key attributes

== Exercise 2: Benchmark models against a chatbot workload

The model performance view estimates serving latency for a given _workload scenario_ and _hardware constraint_, so you can compare candidates on an apples-to-apples basis.

. Enable the **model performance** view in the catalog.

. Configure the workload constraints:
+
* **Scenario:** Chatbot (512 tokens in / 256 tokens out)
* **Latency target:** a TTFT P90 target
* **Max RPS:** the maximum requests-per-second target
+
image::05-performance-workload.png[Workload configuration,link=self,window=blank,width=700]

. Apply a **hardware constraint** -- for example `L4 x1` (a single NVIDIA L4 GPU).
+
The candidate models re-sort by TTFT latency under that hardware.
+
image::06-performance-sorted.png[Models sorted by TTFT,link=self,window=blank,width=700]

. Compare **at least three** validated models by their reported TTFT latency. Note how a larger model may deliver better quality but a higher latency or a larger hardware footprint.

. Identify `{model_name}` among the results.

=== Verify

* The catalog shows a latency-sorted result set under your applied hardware constraint
* You can state the TTFT latency reported for `{model_name}` and at least one other model, and articulate the trade-off between latency, quality, and hardware footprint

=== Verify

== Module summary

**What you accomplished:**

* Filtered the AI hub catalog to Red Hat AI validated models
* Benchmarked candidates against a chatbot workload and a hardware constraint, sorted by TTFT latency
* Selected `{model_name}` and reviewed its model card

**Key takeaways:**

* The AI hub catalog and model performance view let you make a _data-driven_ model choice instead of guessing
* TTFT latency and hardware footprint together determine whether a model fits a chatbot workload on your available GPUs
* Red Hat AI validated models are pre-tested for serving on RHOAI, which reduces deployment risk
