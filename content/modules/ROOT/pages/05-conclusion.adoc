Congratulations! You took a large language model all the way from _discovery_ to a _running, authenticated endpoint_ on Red Hat OpenShift AI -- and consumed it both programmatically and interactively.

== What you accomplished

* **Module 1 -- Discover & benchmark:** filtered the AI hub catalog to Red Hat AI validated models, benchmarked candidates by TTFT latency under a chatbot workload and hardware constraint, and selected `{model_name}`.
* **Module 2 -- Deploy & verify:** deployed the model through the four-step wizard with KServe and the vLLM NVIDIA GPU ServingRuntime on the `{hardware_profile}` GPU profile, then confirmed it reached `Ready` and captured its endpoints and bearer token.
* **Module 3 -- Consume:** called the token-authenticated REST endpoint with `curl`, then chatted with the model in the Gen AI studio Playground and interpreted its inference metrics.

== The end-to-end flow

You connected three surfaces that newcomers usually meet in isolation:

. **Model selection** driven by benchmark data, not guesswork.
. **GPU-backed serving** with KServe/vLLM, external route, and token auth.
. **Consumption** over an OpenAI-compatible API and an interactive Playground.

The same InferenceService now backs both an application integration and a chat experience -- a foundation you can extend to your own models and workloads.

== Where to go next

* Point your own OpenAI-compatible client code at the endpoint by swapping the base URL and token.
* Explore other Red Hat AI validated models and re-run the benchmark comparison for a different workload.
* Learn more about KServe single-model serving, vLLM runtime tuning, and hardware profiles in the RHOAI documentation.
